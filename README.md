# Tesco: Pricing, Profitability & Competitive Strategy

**Strategy Review**

How can Tesco remain price-competitive while protecting sustainable profits?

### Project Overview

This project examines Tesco’s pricing strategy in the context of intensifying competition from discounters (Aldi, Lidl), traditional rivals (Sainsbury’s, Asda), and online/convenience players.

Rather than recommending broad price cuts, the analysis focuses on a disciplined approach:

- Selective price investment only where customer response is measurable
- Test-and-control measurement of incremental contribution
- Funding price investment only from verified productivity savings
- Clear decision rules for scaling or stopping promotions

### Key Insights

- Sales growth significantly outpaced profit growth (+4.6% vs +0.8%), creating a 3.8 percentage point gap that raises questions about the return on recent price investment.

- Strong volume and share gains (UK LFL +4.2%, market share +24bps to 28.5%) indicate that customers are responding to Tesco’s value offer — but the profit impact of that investment remains unclear from public data.
- While management has funded customer investment through Save to Invest (£535m), public disclosures do not isolate the incremental contribution or net profit effect of individual pricing actions.
- A disciplined test-and-learn approach (Design → Test → Scale/Stop) is required to ensure future price investment improves, rather than dilutes, sustainable profitability.


### Approach

1. **Problem definition** – Balancing value perception with contribution economics
2. **Competitive context** – Discounters, traditional competitors, and online
3. **Current strategy assessment** – Aldi Price Match, EDLP, Clubcard, Finest
4. **Evidence boundaries** – What the annual report supports vs. what remains unproven
5. **Diagnostic framework** – The data Tesco would need to decide where price investment pays back
6. **Illustrative economics + pilot design**
7. **Risks and management actions**
